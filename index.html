Executive Summary

A resilient, growing pipeline underpinned by 32 tracked deals

This forecast consolidates every deal in the DSG pipeline — signed contracts, early-stage opportunities and 50%+ probability pipeline — into a single, probability-weighted 5-year view, reported in Rand. Recurring revenue compounds at a steady 30% p.a. once contracts are live; DSG's own revenue (its 50% recurring share plus 100% of one-off build fees) reaches an estimated R8.06M in Year 5, up from R4.13M in Year 1. Clicks and Zamtel have been re-weighted to 80% probability, and Pick n Pay, Global BCP, GoTyme Bank and ABSA South Africa now sit in the 50%+ category — Clicks in particular is close to signature.

Key Takeaways

Underlying recurring revenue is healthy: gross recurring revenue grows a clean 30% every year with no dips.

Year 2 dip in DSG's own revenue is a timing effect: one-off build fees are fully recognized in Year 1 only, inflating Year 1 and creating an optical dip in Year 2 before recurring growth resumes.

Pipeline is broad, not concentrated: 30 pipeline deals (in progress + 50%+) span telecoms, banks and retail across 15+ African markets.

All figures are Rand-denominated: contracts are quoted and collected in ZAR; USD is shown only as a translation for international reference (18.50 ZAR/USD).

Recommendation to the Board

Approve the forecast as the FY2026–FY2031 planning base. Prioritize conversion of the remaining 13 in-progress deals — at 10% probability today, moving them to 50% would add roughly R9.4M to 5-year cumulative DSG revenue. On real Bitdefender wholesale cost terms plus a modeled 20% opex ratio, Year 5 EBITDA reaches an estimated R2.42M (30% margin) — see the Profitability slide for the full bridge and its assumptions.

Why Year 1 is different

Year 1 EBITDA margin (14.2%) is diluted by the same build-fee timing effect seen in Total DSG Revenue: the once-off setup fees carry zero gross margin, so they add revenue and opex load without adding profit. Deals In Progress is EBITDA-negative in Year 1 alone for exactly this reason. From Year 2, margin normalizes to a steady 30%.

FX sensitivity

All contracts are quoted and collected in ZAR — the figures on the rest of this deck are the operating reality. The USD figures on this slide are a translation only, at the planning rate of 18.50 ZAR/USD, for international stakeholders.

A stronger rand (e.g. 16.50) would lift every USD figure shown here by roughly 12%. A weaker rand (e.g. 20.50) would reduce them by roughly 10%. ZAR revenue itself is unaffected by FX movements.

Risk Review

Key risks and sensitivities the Board should weigh

1

Pipeline conversion risk

30 of 32 deals are unsigned. The forecast assumes today's stated probabilities hold (10% in progress; 50% for most 50%+ deals, or 80% for Clicks and Zamtel at higher confidence); slower deal cycles across 15+ markets would push revenue to the right.

High
2

FX translation risk

100% of contracts are ZAR-denominated, which is the Board's primary reporting currency here. A depreciating rand would compress the USD-equivalent figures used for international stakeholder reporting.

Low
3

Client & sector concentration

Every closed and pipeline deal sits on a single product line (Bitdefender-powered security) sold through one channel model — a shock to that partnership affects the whole forecast.

Medium
4

One-off fee timing

Build fees are recognized once, in Year 1 of each deal's close — a cluster of late closings could shift build-fee income into later years than modeled here.

Low
